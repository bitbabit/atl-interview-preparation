Strategy & Business Competencies

Use your real work to show that you think beyond code: client goals, user impact, delivery value, cost, solution options and sensible technology adoption.

How to answer strategy questions

Pattern: Business/user need → technical options → trade-off → decision/contribution → observable value.

Keep examples tied to your actual responsibilities: GraphQL work, release processes, monitoring R&D, code quality and production support.

1. Business Acumen

What Tech Lead level means

Understand client goals, constraints and delivery commitments, then connect technical decisions to business outcomes.

30-second answer

I connect technical work to the business flow it supports. For example, GraphQL caching was not only a performance change. Dynamic TTL was important because time-based coupon behavior still had to remain correct. In production support, I also focus on which user journey is affected so technical priority reflects business impact.

Your evidence

GraphQL caching with dynamic TTL; production-support prioritization by affected flow.

CTO: How do technical decisions connect to business?

Start with the user or operational outcome, then explain how the technical choice supports that outcome.

CTO: How do you prioritize production support work?

Look at affected user journey, business impact, blast radius, workaround and release/delivery risk.

2. Product Thinking

What Tech Lead level means

Think about end-to-end user experience and identify project-level improvement opportunities.

30-second answer

I look beyond the changed module and consider the complete user journey and dependent flows. I use observability and testing impact to understand how a change behaves in the real flow, and synthetic monitoring is another example because it focuses on critical pages and user processes instead of only individual services.

Your evidence

User-journey observability; synthetic monitoring of critical flows.

CTO: How do you think beyond your ticket?

Check upstream/downstream dependencies, the user journey, failure impact and how the changed module contributes to the complete flow.

3. Process Improvement

What Tech Lead level means

Identify project-level improvements and measure whether delivery quality or speed improves.

30-second answer

I converted release review from mainly manual checking into a repeatable workflow. It collects the complete diff, generates module impact and testing reports, creates a register of every changed file, uploads artifacts to Drive and notifies Slack.

Defensible metric today

Every changed file in the generated release diff appears in the register and can be checked for ownership/ticket mapping.

CTO: How do you prove the process adds value?

Track review time, releases processed, unexpected/unmapped files, team adoption and pre-production issues found where measured.

4. Solutioning & Consultative Sales

What Tech Lead level means

Identify improvement opportunities, compare options and contribute to estimation and solution trade-offs.

30-second answer

I contribute by comparing technical options against the project problem. For synthetic monitoring, we evaluated several paid options and also checked a tool the project already had. It covered the required use cases, so it became a practical solution without another purchase.

Your evidence

Synthetic-monitoring R&D; estimation using scope, dependencies, effort, risks and assumptions.

CTO: Client asks for a specific tool. Do you just implement it?

First understand the requirement. Compare approved alternatives and present trade-offs in capability, effort, risk and cost.

5. Economic Thinking

What Tech Lead level means

Balance capability, delivery speed, maintenance, quality and cost.

30-second answer

I compare required capability, implementation effort, maintenance and cost. During synthetic-monitoring R&D, the already purchased tool met the required use cases, so the project could avoid adding another tool cost.

Decision frame

Capability + effort + maintenance + operational risk + cost + value.

CTO: Is the cheapest option always the best?

No. It still has to meet reliability, support and business requirements. Total value matters more than license price alone.

6. Emerging Technologies & Innovation

What Tech Lead level means

Identify project-relevant opportunities and validate them through small R&D/POCs.

30-second answer

I evaluate technology against a real project problem. I define the use case, run a small R&D or POC, compare capability, effort and risk, then decide. My examples are synthetic-monitoring evaluation and the AI-assisted release-review workflow.

Memory line

Problem → R&D/POC → compare → decide → measure.

CTO: How do you avoid innovation for its own sake?

Require a real use case and success criteria. If the new technology does not improve them enough to justify complexity, do not adopt it.

Strategy quick revision

Business Acumen: technical decision → business outcome.

Product Thinking: module → end-to-end user journey.

Process Improvement: repeated pain → repeatable measurable workflow.

Solutioning: compare options and trade-offs.

Economic Thinking: capability + effort + maintenance + cost + value.

Innovation: real problem → small POC → evidence → adoption decision.
