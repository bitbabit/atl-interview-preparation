CTO Practice — All Competencies

Practice short, factual answers based on your actual responsibilities: development, production support, code review, release support, engineering processes, architecture and AI-assisted improvements.

Opening questions

Tell me about yourself.

I’m Babit Kumar, currently working as a Senior Developer at Net Solutions. My main stack is PHP, Magento/Adobe Commerce and GraphQL. On the GSF project I work on backend development, production support and troubleshooting, code review, release support, performance and technical improvements. My scope has moved beyond only implementing tickets toward cross-module impact, release risk, code quality and project-level engineering processes, which is why I’m preparing for the ATL role.

What are your current responsibilities?

Adobe Commerce backend development and GraphQL APIs, code review, production issue analysis using logs/monitoring/database/system signals, release support, cross-module impact analysis, testing coordination and engineering-process improvements such as pre-commit and release-review automation.

Why ATL?

My scope is already moving beyond my own tickets into technical decisions, production and release responsibility, code-review processes, cross-module impact and project-level improvements. ATL is the next step where I can remain hands-on while taking more responsibility for solution impact, delivery risk and team consistency.

Technical competencies

1. Coding & Code Quality — How do you maintain code quality?

Short answer: We use three layers: pre-commit validation, manual plus AI-assisted review, and pre-release full-diff review. My contribution includes project-level review automation and the release-review workflow.

Challenge: Why AI? → It increases review coverage and structures impact analysis; developers remain final reviewers.

Evidence: PHPCS/unit tests, Cursor rules, Python release-review workflow.

2. Testing & QA — How do you know testing is enough?

Short answer: I look at the change, dependencies and affected flows. Developers validate technical behavior and edge cases; QC validates business flows. Before release, impact analysis helps define regression scope.

Evidence: GraphQL cache hit/miss, invalidation, TTL and future-date coupon scenarios.

3. Debugging & Troubleshooting — What is your role in production troubleshooting?

Short answer: I support production troubleshooting by reviewing logs, monitoring, database and system signals, helping narrow the affected layer, coordinating reproduction, supporting the fix/release and verifying the affected flow afterward.

Challenge: Do you always create the RCA? → No. I provide technical evidence and analysis where needed; formal RCA ownership depends on the project process.

Evidence: Production support and observability-based issue analysis.

4. Observability — What does good observability mean to you?

Short answer: Observability should help us detect, understand and verify system behavior. I use logs and monitoring to support production analysis and difficult-to-reproduce issues, and I also worked on synthetic-monitoring R&D for critical user journeys.

5. Software Design & Architecture — How do you make design decisions?

Short answer: Requirement → options → trade-off → decision → verify. I prefer following Adobe Commerce patterns and core behavior before introducing custom logic.

Evidence: GraphQL caching with dynamic TTL and cache invalidation considerations.

6. Security — How do you handle security in development?

Short answer: I follow platform and organization standards, review input handling, authorization, sensitive-data exposure and dependency impact. AI is only an additional review signal; final validation remains human.

7. AI-Assisted Development — How are you using AI beyond autocomplete?

Short answer: I use AI for repeatable engineering workflows. I created project/Magento review rules and built a Python + Cursor release workflow that analyzes actual release artifacts, generates impact/testing outputs and creates team-visible reports.

8. Prompt Engineering & Workflow Design — What makes your prompt workflow reliable?

Short answer: Fixed prompts, project/Magento rules, real Git artifacts and a structured output format. That makes the result repeatable and easier to validate.

9. AI Quality & Compliance — How do you trust AI output?

Short answer: I don’t treat AI as source of truth. Source code and Git artifacts remain the source. AI findings are validated by developers before action.

10. Data Management — How do you approach database-related issues?

Short answer: I first review evidence such as query behavior, access patterns, node load, indexes and logs before recommending a change. The goal is to understand the actual bottleneck instead of changing queries or schema by assumption.

11. Certifications — Why is your certification score lower?

Short answer: My recent focus has been project delivery, Adobe Commerce, GraphQL, production support and engineering automation. I would state my certification status directly and only commit to a timeline if I have a defined plan.

Strategy & business competencies

12. Business Acumen — How do technical decisions connect to business?

Short answer: I connect technical work to the affected business flow. GraphQL caching, for example, had to improve repeated processing while keeping time-based coupon behavior correct.

Challenge: How do you prioritize production work? → By affected user journey, business impact, blast radius, workaround and delivery risk.

13. Product Thinking — How do you think beyond your ticket?

Short answer: I consider the full user journey and dependent flows, not only the changed module. Observability and synthetic monitoring are useful because they look at real user journeys rather than isolated endpoints.

14. Process Improvement — What process have you improved?

Short answer: I converted release review into a repeatable workflow that collects the complete diff, generates impact/testing reports, creates a change register and shares the artifacts with the team.

Challenge: How do you measure value? → Review time, releases processed, unexpected/unmapped files, adoption and issues caught before production where measured.

15. Solutioning & Consultative Sales — How do you add value beyond assigned work?

Short answer: I compare technical options against the project problem and explain scope, dependencies, effort, risks and assumptions. Synthetic-monitoring R&D is a good example.

16. Economic Thinking — How do you consider cost?

Short answer: I compare capability, implementation effort, maintenance, operational risk and cost. During monitoring R&D, an already purchased tool met the required use cases, so there was no need to add another tool cost.

17. Emerging Technologies & Innovation — How do you evaluate new technology?

Short answer: Problem first → small R&D/POC → compare capability, effort and risk → decide. I avoid adopting technology only because it is new.

Delivery & leadership competencies

18. Delivery Planning — How do you plan a feature?

Short answer: Requirement → task breakdown → dependencies → estimate → risks → sequence. If an assumption changes, I raise the impact early.

19. Reliability & Accountability — What does ownership mean to you?

Short answer: Ownership means following work through development, review, release and production verification—not only completing the code.

Evidence: Release support, file ownership/ticket mapping and post-deployment verification.

20. Collaboration & Communication — How do you coordinate with developers, QC and leads?

Short answer: I keep communication tied to action: affected flow, evidence, testing need, owner and next step. That makes the information useful to each role.

21. Conflict Resolution — What do you do when developers disagree?

Short answer: Bring the discussion back to the requirement and compare options using platform standards, impact, effort and maintainability. If still unresolved, summarize the trade-offs and involve the appropriate lead.

22. Knowledge Sharing — How do you share knowledge?

Short answer: I try to make knowledge reusable through project rules, review templates, release reports, shared artifacts and explanations to developers—not only verbal discussions.

23. Engineering Process Adherence — Which processes do you follow and improve?

Short answer: Git/commit standards, code review, Sonar, CI/CD and testing gates. I added earlier project checks such as pre-commit validation and release review; these complement shared gates rather than replace them.

24. Compliance & Governance — How do you ensure controls are followed?

Short answer: I follow approved security, access and delivery processes, keep credentials/config out of committed code and maintain change traceability. If a requirement is unclear, I confirm it instead of assuming.

Cross-competency CTO challenges

What production responsibilities show you are ready for ATL?

Production support, release impact review, technical issue analysis, cross-module thinking, coordination with QC/developers, post-release verification and improving the engineering processes around those activities.

What would you improve first as ATL?

Choose one real project problem such as release-risk visibility, code-review consistency, critical-journey monitoring or testing impact. Explain the expected value and how you would measure it.

Business wants speed, engineering wants quality. What do you do?

Make the trade-off explicit. Protect non-negotiables such as security/data integrity, then adjust scope, rollout strategy or sequencing to meet the business need safely.

What metrics would you track?

Delivery predictability, production issues/defects, release leakage, review time, testing effectiveness, performance indicators and measurable outcomes from engineering-process improvements.
